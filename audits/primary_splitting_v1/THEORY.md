# Primary splitting explains the target-phase-free lifting step

27 September 2026. Contribution audit, not manuscript text. The following
lemmas are elementary consequences of finite-group primary decomposition.
They are written out to make a matched comparison possible, not asserted as
new group theory. The existing source compiler and its proofs remain unchanged.

## 1. An abstract lemma, independent of loops and coordinates

Let pi:G -> Q be a homomorphism of finite groups whose kernel is a p-group.
Let g,h in G commute. Write ord(pi(g)) = m p^b with gcd(m,p)=1. Then

    h belongs to <g>
      iff pi(h) belongs to <pi(g)> and h^m belongs to <g^m>.       (1)

The same statement holds if m is any prime-to-p multiple of the prime-to-p
part of ord(pi(g)). This is not a method for testing either condition in an
arbitrary black-box group; the computational representation still matters.

Proof. The forward implication is immediate. For the reverse implication,
choose t with pi(h)=pi(g)^t and put k=h g^(-t). This is a kernel element, so
its order is a power of p. The order of g^m is also a power of p: its image
has p-power order and any additional factor must come from the p-kernel.
If h^m=(g^m)^z, commutation gives k^m=(g^m)^(z-t). Choose p^a divisible by the
orders of k and g^m, and choose s with ms=1 modulo p^a. Then

    k=(k^m)^s belongs to <g^m>,

and h=g^t k belongs to <g>. No value of t need be computed. QED.

For the previous module result, G may be restricted to the finite abelian group
generated by the two commuting automorphisms C and B=h(C). The reduction map is
on M/pM. Its kernel is a p-group, as already proved in the preceding note.
Cyclicity of the initial module turns accepted residue-state membership into
residue-ENDOMORPHISM membership. These hypotheses are not optional.

Consequently the target-phase-free equivalence in Section 6 of
../../research/prime_power_compilation_v1/THEORY.md is a direct instance of (1).
The mixed-module construction makes that instance executable; it does not
change the group-theoretic reason the unknown exponent can be removed.

## 2. An alternative using ordinary Chinese-remainder projectors

A matched alternative can expose the conventional primary decomposition
explicitly. Suppose a p-power P bounds the p-part of every target automorphism
that passes the residue test, as well as that of the source. Then both g and h
have order dividing E=mP. Choose the integer projectors

    e_s = 0 mod P,  e_s = 1 mod m,
    e_p = 1-e_s mod E.

For m=1 use e_s=0. For P=1 the formulas give e_p=0. For E=1 both components
are the identity. Compute these exponents by the extended Euclidean algorithm;
no factorization or discrete logarithm is required. Set

    g_s=g^(e_s), g_p=g^(e_p), h_s=h^(e_s), h_p=h^(e_p).

The s-parts have orders dividing m, the p-parts have orders dividing P, and
h=h_s h_p, g=g_s g_p. If pi(h) belongs to <pi(g)>, then pi(h_s) belongs to
<pi(g_s)>. In the commuting group generated by g and h, the quotient kernel
has p-power order, whereas h_s g_s^(-t) has order dividing m. Their intersection
is trivial. Thus h_s belongs to <g_s>. It follows that

    h belongs to <g> iff h_p belongs to <g_p>                (2)

AFTER the residue-membership test. The independent choices of exponents in
the two primary subgroups combine by CRT because their orders are coprime.

The audit adapter replaces only the prior h^m test by this projector version.
It gives the same all-target membership contract, using exactly the same
checked source certificate and the same polynomial-time p-group primitive.
It is a derived standard-algebra comparator, not a retrieved implementation of
a full earlier compiler. It cannot establish the priority of the earlier field
compiler by assuming that compiler as its own input.

## 3. A target-order bound is essential

For a module M killed by p^e, a kernel automorphism K=I+D has D(M) inside pM.
The binomial identity implies K^(p^(e-1))=I; the induction works for p=2 as
well. If ord(Cbar)=r0=m p^b and Bbar belongs to <Cbar>, then B^r0 lies in the
kernel. Thus ord(B) divides m p^(b+e-1). A valid source-only bound is

    P=p^(b+e-1).

Its bit length is polynomial in the explicit source size. This bound does
not require knowing the target's actual order or its exponent in <Cbar>.
Residue invertibility first establishes that B is an automorphism of M.

Using only the p-part of the SOURCE period as a bound for B can be unsound.
Consider x -> -x modulo 25 from 1. Its orbit is {1,24} and period is 2, with
no factor 5. The target 6 reduces to 1 modulo 5 and so passes the residue test.
Nevertheless it is not in the orbit. The source-only bound P=1 would make
e_p=0 and erase every target's p-component. The correct admissible-target bound
P=5 retains the obstruction. Both the original query and the correct projector
adapter reject 6. This is a rejected shortcut, NOT a bug in the existing code.

An abstract form is C2 x C5 -> C2, with g=(1,0) and h=(1,1). The order of g
is 2 but h has a nontrivial 5-component. This explains the issue independently
of the normalization routine or any particular coordinate convention.

## 4. Two other hypotheses have explicit counterexamples

Commutation cannot be removed from (1). In S3, map to C2 by the sign map. The
kernel A3 is a 3-group. Let g=(12), h=(23), and m=2. Both have the same quotient
image, and h^2=g^2=1, but h does not belong to <g>. They do not commute.
The orbit implementation has commutation because B is a polynomial in C.

The kernel cannot contain arbitrary other-primary torsion. For G=C4 x C2,
pi the first-coordinate projection, and p=3, take g=(1,0), h=(0,1), m=4.
The quotient condition holds and h^4=g^4=1, yet h is not in <g>. The kernel
is a 2-group, not a 3-group. This is why applying the one-characteristic
argument to unrelated prime factors is not automatically sound.

## 5. Matched costs and exact scope

Both paths receive the SAME original source and serialized certificate. They
share the independently checked cyclic-module normalization, the field-source
compiler, and the characteristic-primary membership routine. This deliberately
holds those layers fixed to compare the lifting reduction itself.

Preparing the alternative adds one CRT calculation, one source power, and an
exact p-order computation. At query time it performs the same coordinate map,
residue membership and target-endomorphism construction, one binary power with
a polynomial-bit exponent, and the same p-group primitive. Thus it has the same
coarse polynomial size/verification/query guarantees and needs no new certificate
data or target finite-field logarithm. No better asymptotic exponent or practical
running-time claim is made. Construction costs for the common field certificate
are not erased. Additional certificate bytes zero means the existing certificate
is REUSED, not that the full algorithm has no proof input.

This resolves a narrow comparison: target-phase removal in the prime-power
wrapper is not an adequate standalone novelty claim. It does not prove the
whole source-certificate theorem has appeared in a specific previous paper.
That theorem still needs a comparison at the verified representation level.

## 6. Executed falsification and comparison

The audit compiles 1,408 sources and checks 25,068 full-state queries against
ordinary independently stepped trajectories. It includes 370 sources with
mixed generator orders. The projector adapter and existing predicate agree on
all 6,698 reachable and 18,370 unreachable queries. A separate 294 queries reuse
six previously saved source certificates, including the large closed-form cases.

Abstract additive groups provide 40,060 source/target checks over 540 generators
of groups C_m x C_(p^a) x C_(p^b), with p=2 or 3, m at most 6 and coprime to p,
and (a,b)=(1,1) or (2,1). This checks the group lemma with a separate finite
oracle, not just the shared endomorphism primitive. The counterexamples above
and malformed CRT factors are retained. Query-only controls disable source
normalization and irreducibility checking and reject producer imports.

Finite agreement is not a proof of Python correctness. In particular, this
adapter is not an independent implementation of the shared p-group decoder,
module normalizer, or residue compiler. Earlier independent module/function
controls remain useful and are not relabelled as new comparison evidence.

## 7. Scientific assessment

The preceding source theorem remains correct within its proved scope. Its
implementation now has an additional mathematical cross-check. But the apparent
new 'remove the unknown time' mechanism is primary decomposition, and its
prime-power extension should be treated as a supported corollary/application,
not as the principal novelty claim on current evidence.

No manuscript or new public API is introduced. Further domain extensions are
not the next priority. The remaining research target is a specific independently
checkable source-representation guarantee, together with its closest compact,
certifying predecessor and a justified benefit. The source comparison below
separates that obligation from what this controlled adapter actually settles.
